# The 3-Pass Reading Method

Source: [UBC iSchool - How To Read an Academic Paper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SKxm2HF_-k0)

---

Academic papers are not written like short stories or newspaper articles. Reading them beginning to end is inefficient. Instead, read in three passes.

---

## Pass 1: Framework (5-10 min)

**What to read:**
- Abstract
- Introduction
- Conclusion
- Section headings
- Tables and diagrams (including captions)

**What to do:**
- Underline unfamiliar words or phrases (look up later)
- Ask yourself:
  - What is the thesis of this paper?
  - What are the main arguments?
  - Why is this paper important?
  - How does it contribute to the field?
- Write down any questions that come up

**Goal:** Summarize the paper in 1-2 sentences in your own words.

If you can do that, you got what you need from Pass 1.

---

## Pass 2: Deep Dive (30-60 min)

**What to read:**
- Beginning and ending of each major section
- Highlighted sections from Pass 1
- Look up words/phrases you still don't understand

**What to do:**
- Answer the questions you wrote down in Pass 1

**Goal:** Understand the key points, not every detail.

---

## Pass 3: Critical Analysis (30-60 min)

**What to read:**
- Full paper with a critical eye

**What to do:**
- Take notes on arguments, evidence, conclusions
- Use your own words (don't copy-paste quotes)
- Answer these questions (depending on paper type):
  - Did the authors do what they set out to do?
  - Are the methods sound?
  - Are the arguments fluid and logical?
  - What assumptions did they make?

**Goal:** Form your own view. Have the basis for a summary.

---

## Note

This method assumes you've already selected a worthwhile paper. If you're doing research (not assigned reading), you'll need to evaluate whether a paper is worth your time first.
